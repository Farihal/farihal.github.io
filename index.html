R3Arm: gesture-controlled rescue arm

2017 · Co-authorIEEE R10-HTC

A low-cost, modular robotic arm for remote rescue work, driven by hand gestures instead of a joystick so an operator can control it naturally from a distance.

  • 3-DOF arm on a mobile base, controlled by finger-mounted accelerometers.
  • Gesture data sent wirelessly over an nRF24L01 link.
  • Live smartphone video lets the operator work without line of sight.

My role: gesture acquisition, firmware, mechanical design.

P. P. Sarker, F. Abedin, and F. N. Shimim, “R3Arm: Gesture controlled robotic arm for remote rescue operation,” Proc. IEEE Region 10 Humanitarian Technology Conf. (R10-HTC), 2017, pp. 428–431.

R3Diver: underwater rescue ROV

2016 · Co-authorIEEE TENCON

A remotely operated vehicle for underwater search and rescue, built for about USD 100 so that rescue teams with small budgets could afford one.

  • PVC frame, four thrusters and 3D-printed propellers.
  • Wired USB-over-CAT6 control with live video down to 30 m.
  • Automatic human detection in the video feed using HOG and Haar cascade classifiers.

My role: control, thruster design, HMI, vision.

S. A. Fattah, F. Abedin, M. N. Ansary, M. A. Rokib, N. Saha, and C. Shahnaz, “R3Diver: Remote robotic rescue diver for rapid underwater search and rescue operation,” Proc. IEEE TENCON, 2016, pp. 3280–3283.

Accident risk from live traffic video

Computer vision · OpenCVVehicle-to-vehicle & vehicle-to-human

A computer-vision system that watches a live traffic camera and estimates how likely vehicles are to hit each other or a pedestrian, so a warning can go out before the accident happens.

  • Detects vehicles and people in each frame of a live video feed and tracks them as they move, using OpenCV.
  • Measures how close each pair of moving objects gets and turns that into a collision probability.
  • Covers both vehicle-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-human accidents.

Ride-hailing for flood rescue

Mobile app · Disaster responseFloods

An app that works like Uber for flood rescue: stranded people request help from their phones, and the nearest available rescue team is sent to them.

  • People caught in a flood send an SOS with their location.
  • The app matches each request with the nearest free rescue team, the way a ride-hailing app matches a rider with a driver.
  • Both sides can follow the rescue as it happens, so teams don't duplicate work and families know help is coming.

Electric motor for rural river boats

2023 – 2024 · M.Sc. thesisFirst author

A 12 kW interior permanent-magnet motor designed as a drop-in replacement for the single-cylinder diesel engines that power boats on Bangladesh's inland waterways.

>93%efficiency at rated and peak torque (target 92%)
59%less peak cogging torque from three-step rotor skew, 1.672 → 0.675 N·m
93.81%energy utilization over the ISO 8178-4 E5 duty cycle
7.85rotor safety factor at 110% of maximum speed
  • Designed in ANSYS Motor-CAD: 48 slots, 8 poles, V-shaped NdFeB magnets and water-jacket cooling, matched to the legacy engines' 2200 rpm operating point.
  • Electromagnetic, mechanical and thermal analysis. Peak cogging torque 1.3% of rated, torque ripple 7.32%, 52.10 N·m at 2200 rpm.
  • Built from 180 mm stator and 114 mm rotor laminations of M19 steel, with an 87 mm stack, a 1 mm air gap, N42UH sintered NdFeB magnets and eight rotor cooling ducts.

F. Abedin and M. Z. R. Khan, “Design and Analysis of a Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor for Sustainable Rural Water Transportation,” Next Research, 2026, p. 101589. Supervisor: Prof. Md. Ziaur Rahman Khan.

Wearable control of soft magnetic microrobots

Project · Human-in-the-loop controlMinimally invasive surgery

A project on steering soft magnetic microrobots inside the body with a forearm-worn armband instead of a console. The surgeon's hand gestures are decoded from muscle signals, turned into magnetic fields, and checked against live X-ray images.

  • Four parts working together: a modular soft microrobot, built from small hydrogel units carrying magnetic particles that move as one body, a wearable armband that reads muscle signals and wrist motion, electromagnetic coils around the patient, and X-ray fluoroscopy to see the robot.
  • Three gesture commands: steer moves the robot along the surgeon's pointing direction, hold pins it against the vessel wall, and release delivers its payload at the target.
  • Designed around delay: the surgeon steers from an X-ray image that is always slightly out of date, and each gesture takes time to decode. Gestures the decoder isn't sure about default to hold, and the robot holds automatically if imaging is lost.
